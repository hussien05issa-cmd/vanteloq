# Dealership operations: research, implementation and remaining boundaries

Reviewed October 3, 2026. This report combines the full supplied dealership and retail brief, the existing Vanteloq implementation, and the official sources linked below. It is implementation guidance, not a statement that a dealership or this software satisfies every regulatory requirement.

## Decision

The existing vehicle register is a sound, scoped stock register, but a mutable “sold” status is insufficient evidence for delivered sales, salesperson attribution, historical inventory or accounting revenue. The first useful increment is an additive vehicle identity and stock episode model with explicit delivery events, task and customer workflows, reviewed cost evidence, and a visible boundary between operational figures and authoritative accounting records.

That bounded foundation has now been implemented in `domain/dealership.ts`, `server/dealership.ts`, `app/api/v1/dealership/route.ts`, `db/dealership-schema.ts` and the registered `drizzle/0070_dealership_operations.sql` migration. The original vehicle table remains separate. The integration lane owns industry configuration, migrations and publishing; a separate UI lane owns the dealership workspace. No dealership provider connection or legal certification is implied. Final integrated acceptance is tracked in [the acceptance record](../industry-expansion-acceptance-2026-10-03.md).

## Operational model and calculations

**Identity and stock episodes.** One organization-scoped vehicle identity can have successive stock episodes. There can be only one active episode per vehicle and active stock number. The episode preserves acquisition date, location name, currency and source. A transfer changes physical business responsibility without resetting acquisition age. A reversal preserves the original delivery and does not silently assert that the vehicle has physically returned. Reacquisition is a separate reviewed operation.

**Independent states.** Ownership (owned, consignment, unknown), physical position, preparation and availability are independent. A vehicle can be consigned, offsite and blocked at the same time. An active reservation is exclusive at the database level. “Available” summary eligibility requires recorded available status, on-lot position, preparation ready, and known owned/consignment classification. This remains an operational state, not an AMVIC compliance certificate.

**Preparation.** Assignable tasks support open, in progress, blocked and done, with due dates and explicit block reasons. A task completion is not a supplier invoice or accounting posting. The vendor research supports separate preparation workflow, cost review and responsibility: vAuto describes task templates, individual line-item approvals and internal coordination in [iRecon](https://www.vauto.com/products/irecon/). Its commercial claims are not performance benchmarks for Vanteloq.

**Costs and operational gross.** Acquisition, auction, transport, preparation and other cost lines preserve their state: recorded legacy amount, estimated, approved or posted evidence. Only posted lines enter the current cost subtotal; coverage must be explicitly reviewed. Delivery freezes that reviewed owned-stock subtotal. Missing coverage remains null, including consignment gross. Later estimates cannot rewrite a completed delivery. “Posted” here is a user's source-backed classification and does not mean Vanteloq posted to a general ledger.

Accounting treatment requires an entity-specific policy. Where IFRS applies, [IAS 2](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-2-inventories/) describes inventory at the lower of cost and net realizable value, costs of bringing inventory to its location and condition, and specific identification for non-interchangeable items. This does not establish that every Canadian private dealership reports under IFRS or that management holding-cost estimates are capitalizable. ASPE and the entity's applicable reporting framework require separate review.

**Delivered sales and credit.** One recorded delivery equals one vehicle regardless of how many people receive credit. The recorded vehicle amount excludes invented payment, tax, finance and product proceeds. Approved credit shares use basis points, sum to at most 100%, and leave the remainder explicitly unassigned. Largest-remainder allocation preserves every cent, including negative gross. A 60/40 split of 1,200,001 cents allocates 720,001 and 480,000 cents but remains one delivery. This allocation is a product rule, not a claimed universal dealership standard or commission agreement.

Vendor CRM research distinguishes staff activity and outcomes. [VinSolutions' team dashboard explanation](https://www.vinsolutions.com/resources/blog/quick-tip-team-performance-dashboard-fundamentals/) discusses leads, appointments, visits and sales; [its customer-success documentation](https://www.vinsolutions.com/dedicated-to-your-success/) describes reconciling CRM records with DMS sales/customer data. The recommendation is to record lead and appointment events separately and reconcile deliveries, not to infer a salesperson's sale from a lead's present assignee.

**Scope and dates.** Inventory counts use the entire authorized location and search scope, not the first 100 displayed rows. Sales summaries use the entire selected delivery/reversal period and separate currencies. Delivery activity and dated reversals are reported separately, not labelled net accounting revenue. Two-decimal currency support is explicit; new records and legacy adoption reject zero- and three-decimal currencies rather than misrepresenting minor units. CSV exports either include the full permitted scope, up to 5,000 records, or explicitly require a narrower selection.

## Canadian and Alberta requirements to inform later checkpoints

### Federal GST/HST and trade-ins

CRA distinguishes a trade-in supplied by a customer who must charge GST/HST from a trade-in where the customer is not required to charge it. The former generally involves separate taxable supplies; the latter can use the trade-in allowance in the taxable price calculation. Registration alone is insufficient to decide the facts. CRA also distinguishes consignment agency from purchase/resale arrangements and explains deposit treatment. [CRA: special cases](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-special-cases.html)

Recommended later fields are selling amount before tax, trade allowance, separate vehicle acquisition basis, customer tax treatment/evidence, tax jurisdiction/date/rate, lien payout and the actual accounting journal reference. For an illustrative Alberta sale of $30,000 with a $10,000 eligible non-taxable-customer trade allowance at 5%, tax on the $20,000 difference is $1,000. In the separate taxable-supplies case, the same nominal amounts can involve $1,500 charged on the dealer sale and $500 on the customer's supply, subject to the actual facts and input tax credit eligibility. Do not treat lien payoff, allowance, fair value and dealer cost as interchangeable.

No GST/HST, trade-in tax engine, automatic journal or financing calculation has been enabled by this increment.

### Alberta advertised pricing and vehicle history

AMVIC's February 2025 guidance says advertised prices must include applicable required fees, attached accessories and other stated charges, with GST and financing costs excluded from the all-in advertised price rule. A disclaimer does not authorize adding mandatory charges contrary to that rule. A future merchandising model should retain the advertised price, mandatory components and effective version rather than representing an incomplete base amount as compliant. [AMVIC: website disclaimers and all-in pricing](https://www.amvic.org/website-disclaimers-and-all-in-advertised-pricing/)

Alberta's official guidance describes a mechanical fitness assessment for used vehicles, completion by the designated licensed journeyperson, and 120-day validity. AMVIC distinguishes this assessment from a vehicle inspection. Future readiness checkpoints should preserve document issue date, technician/source, validity date and when the customer received it. The complete legal applicability and any exceptions should be reviewed before enforcing a universal gate. [Alberta: mechanical fitness assessments](https://www.alberta.ca/mechanical-fitness-assessments-for-used-vehicles), [AMVIC: understanding vehicle history](https://www.amvic.org/buying-used-understanding-vehicle-history/)

AMVIC's research guidance identifies disclosures including relevant prior use, certain damage or branded history, prior jurisdiction and incident repair costs exceeding the stated threshold. Use yes/no/unknown with dated evidence, not false defaults. Absence of an entry in a report is not proof that an event never occurred. [AMVIC: researching the vehicle](https://www.amvic.org/business/licence-responsibilities-information/researching-the-vehicle/)

Alberta's Vehicle Information Report covers Alberta records and may show a lien count. Detailed lien searches use the Personal Property Registry separately. A VIR is neither a national vehicle history report nor a blanket lien-clearance certificate. The product should retain report jurisdiction, date and source and keep lien verification separate. [Alberta: Vehicle Information Report](https://www.alberta.ca/vehicle-information-report)

### Privacy, consent and retention

For Alberta private-sector activity, PIPA governs covered personal information; purposes, reasonable collection and consent matter. Alberta also explains notice requirements relevant to service providers outside Canada. Do not describe hosting or data residency as Canadian unless that is actually established. [Alberta: PIPA overview](https://www.alberta.ca/personal-information-protection-act-overview), [Alberta: collecting personal information](https://www.alberta.ca/collecting-personal-information)

Federal PIPEDA remains relevant to covered commercial personal information crossing provincial or national borders, even where a province has substantially similar private-sector legislation. Provincial and federal applicability are distinct questions. [Office of the Privacy Commissioner: PIPEDA in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)

This increment stores limited customer name/contact references under customer-identity permissions and sends no messages. It does not establish marketing consent, a CASL workflow or consent to disclose documents. Avoid SINs, identity-document images and lender applications in free-text fields. A later communication feature needs a separate lawful-purpose, consent, retention and suppression design.

CRA generally requires relevant GST/HST records to be retained for six years from the end of the year to which they relate, with qualifications and possible longer periods. This supports a records policy with retention classes and holds, rather than deleting financial evidence when a staff account or location disappears. [CRA: records to keep](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/calculate-prepare-report/gst-hst-records-keep.html)

New user-deletion behaviour preserves shared sale amounts and credit shares, clears deleted-user foreign keys, and replaces the person's credit name with “Former member.” Customer records are business records with a separate lifecycle; deleting a team login is not a request to erase an unrelated customer.

## Further lanes, in priority order

1. **Built route and rendered workflow verification.** Exercise industry and capability gates, paid entitlement, MFA, origin protection, live role revocation, location restrictions, exports and empty states. Verify record creation through the UI before making availability claims. Current focused server tests use real SQLite through a D1-compatible test adapter; they do not replace built Worker verification.
2. **Record detail and provenance.** Add visible immutable event history, task transition timestamps, document attachments and reviewed disclosure checkpoints. Keep no-history labels for adopted legacy records. A snapshot imported as already sold must not acquire an invented delivered date.
3. **Source reconciliation.** Add DMS/provider adapters only after access, mapping and source authority are configured. [PBS's partner programme](https://www.pbssystems.com/partners) requires an integration relationship; a product name in a provider selector is not a working integration. Manual and provider facts need conflict review and stable external event IDs.
4. **Floorplan.** Introduce lender facility, VIN/episode advance, principal movements, lender fees, curtailment due dates and payout evidence. Keep contractual balances separate from daily holding-cost estimates. The Canadian IAA/AFC page describes inventory financing and repayment as stock sells, subject to provider approval. Product structures can differ: an older OPENLANE release described a daily-fee alternative. Neither supports a universal APR or current fee default. [IAA Canada: AFC](https://ca.iaai.com/marketing/afc), [OPENLANE's historical product announcement](https://corporate.openlane.com/afc-empowers-cash-buyers-with-flexible-alternative-to-traditional-floorplan-solutions/)
5. **F&I and trade-ins.** Record product revenue, product/provider cost, receivables, lender reserve, funded date, cancellation/chargeback events and agreed sales-credit policy separately. A finance amount is not dealer revenue; a lender approval is not funding. Link the trade-in as its own stock episode with allowance, purchase basis and lien evidence separated. Review tax treatment with current authoritative guidance before automating it.
6. **Aging and cohort measures.** Use real acquisition and event dates. Separate active-stock age from completed days to delivery; disclose unknown acquisition dates and comparable populations. Report retail and wholesale independently when the metric's meaning requires it. Do not average rows duplicated by credit splits or mix currencies. Procurement decisions require actual licensed market data; [vAuto Provision](https://www.vauto.com/products/provision/) illustrates the relevant workflow but does not supply Vanteloq with valuation data.

## Verification and limits

`tests/dealership.test.ts` passes seven focused tests spanning validators, exact allocation, imports, stock scope and exports, transactional reservations, delivery/reversal history, cost evidence/redaction, legacy adoption, customer tasks/appointments, tenant isolation, deletion behaviour and an interleaved transfer/task-write race. The command is `node scripts/test.mjs tests/dealership.test.ts` from `work/vanteloq` using the bundled Node runtime. The final run completed in 3.9 seconds, with no build, network or provider calls.

`tests/dealership-flow.test.mjs` passed its built Worker integration test, covering reviewed industry changes, inventory-only role redaction, location boundaries, scoped CSV exports, live role revocation, MFA, origin checks, paid access cancellation and preservation of stored sales when the industry changes. It passed against the initial build; the final small stock-task race guard and any later integrated changes require the final build verification owned by the parent release lane.

Posted cost evidence is append-only in this increment. A reviewed correction/void workflow, late cost amendments after delivery and corresponding accounting adjustments are deferred; users should verify source references and amounts before recording posted evidence. This limitation is material and the workspace must not be presented as a complete accounting cost subledger.

The foundation intentionally does not claim automatic appraisal, VIN-history retrieval, tax filing, ledger posting, lender integration, financing approval, external messaging, compliance certification, a commission payroll engine or complete DMS replacement. Those are independently testable future capabilities, not missing values that should be filled with assumptions.
