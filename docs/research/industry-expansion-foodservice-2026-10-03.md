# Foodservice calculations and implementation boundary

Research checked October 3, 2026, for cafés, restaurants and relevant food retail. This is operational analysis, not an accounting posting, nutrition calculator or food-safety certification. The supplied dealership/retail master brief requires governed definitions, explicit currencies, matching scope, source coverage and honest missing-data handling. The foodservice extension applies those principles to a bounded recipe and period-review workflow.

## Research and definitions

**Recipe yield and units.** Purchased quantity and usable prepared quantity are different measures. Ingredient cost must use the quantity and food form described by the purchase and recipe. USDA explains that its food yields are research-based averages, affected by product condition and preparation; its guidance supports documenting observed yields. Its school-meal approval rules are specific to that programme and are not imposed on Canadian restaurants here. The module requires an explicit prepared/purchased yield factor, including documented gains from hydration. It does not assume that 1 gram equals 1 millilitre. [USDA Food Buying Guide](https://foodbuyingguide.fns.usda.gov/Home/About), [USDA Appendix B](https://foodbuyingguide.fns.usda.gov/Appendix/ResourceAppendixB).

The BC Cook Articulation Committee describes yield testing as a way to determine usable portions and their cost. Vanteloq's recipe formula is `pack cost × prepared quantity used ÷ (pack quantity × preparation yield)`, summed over ingredients, then divided by the recipe's actual yielded portions. Inputs must name purchase and recipe units and use a consistent food form. Unknown yield or purchase cost makes the result unavailable. [BCcampus, Yield Testing](https://opentextbc.ca/basickitchenandfoodservicemanagement/chapter/yield-testing/).

**Exact unit conversion.** The implemented mass units are grams, kilograms, avoirdupois pounds and explicitly named mass ounces. One pound is exactly 0.45359237 kg; a mass ounce is one sixteenth of a pound. Volume conversions are limited to litres and millilitres, and count uses `each`. Ambiguous cups, fluid ounces and gallons are excluded until an explicit measurement system is supplied. Mass-to-volume and count-to-mass conversion require ingredient-specific density or pack evidence and are rejected by this release. [NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C](https://www.nist.gov/document/2026-nist-handbook-44-appendix-c).

**Actual food depletion.** Beginning inventory plus purchases minus ending inventory is the basic cost-flow calculation. Credits and transfers require adjustment; employee meals and other uses may need classification outside the selected food-sales scope. The implementation uses `opening + purchases − supplier credits + transfers in − transfers out − closing`, all at one reviewed valuation basis. Recorded waste is already within depletion and must not be added again. Separate classifications remain a review responsibility. A negative result is flagged for review, not replaced with zero. [BCcampus, Monthly Food Costs](https://opentextbc.ca/basickitchenandfoodservicemanagement/chapter/monthly-food-costs/).

**Theoretical cost and variance.** Standard recipe cost multiplied by eligible menu-item/portion counts gives theoretical cost. Recipe versions, yields and ingredient prices must match the analytical period. The actual-minus-theoretical difference is a numerical variance, not proof of waste or theft. Toast's own AVT documentation describes recipe-based theoretical usage and the broader measured actual usage. Refund quantities do not automatically restore consumed ingredients. The pure domain accepts explicit prepared/eligible portions. The saved period form currently accepts a separately reviewed theoretical-cost total; it does not claim automatic POS or recipe-version matching. [BCcampus, Interpreting POS Information](https://opentextbc.ca/basickitchenandfoodservicemanagement/chapter/interpret-point-of-sale-information/), [Toast, Actual versus Theoretical](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/xtraCHEF-Get-Started-With-Actual-vs-Theoretical-Analysis-Reports).

**Waste.** EPA's assessment resources support recording the amount, type and source of wasted food. Vanteloq's selected operational definition is recorded waste quantity divided by available quantity, or recorded waste cost divided by available inventory cost for the same scope. These are separate measures. Cost weighting is required before combining unlike foods; kilograms and individual items cannot be summed. The implementation flags waste exceeding availability and does not label all unexplained variance as recorded waste. [EPA, Resources for Assessing Wasted Food](https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-management-food/resources-assessing-wasted-food).

**Labour percentage.** Toast documents labour cost as a percentage of net sales in its labour reporting. Vanteloq requires a reviewed cost total and matching total net sales. The employer must state whether the numerator includes wages only or also payroll burden, benefits and allocated salary. The module does not calculate wages, deductions or employment entitlements. Missing labour cost remains unavailable; food sales alone must not silently become the denominator for the broader total-sales ratio. [Toast, Labour Reports Overview](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Labor-Reports-Overview).

**Contribution.** Menu contribution is selling price less food cost, the amount available for labour, operating costs and profit. It is not restaurant net profit. The period report labels food contribution as net food sales less actual food depletion. A separate pure calculation subtracts explicitly supplied additional variable costs, avoiding costs already included in depletion. No margin or waste benchmark is assumed. [BCcampus, Principles of Menu Engineering](https://opentextbc.ca/basickitchenandfoodservicemanagement/chapter/describe-the-principles-of-menu-engineering/).

**Checks, guests and table turns.** Average check uses matching tax-exclusive net sales divided by eligible closed checks. Guest counts require separate captured evidence and are not inferred from checks. Table turns use parties served divided by available tables in a named service window with a consistent table configuration. Split payments/checks must not multiply seated parties. Turn duration is a different measure requiring seating and clearing timestamps. The domain supports the turn ratio; the first saved workspace does not yet collect seating-event records or display a turn-time claim. [Toast, Guest Counts](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Calculate-Guest-Count), [Toast, Table Turnover](https://pos.toasttab.com/uk/blog/on-the-line/the-science-of-table-turnover-faster-service-and-happier-guests).

## Implemented contract and controls

`domain/foodservice.ts` contains independent pure calculations. Monetary inputs are safe integer minor units with explicit currency. Quantities use decimal strings with at most six decimal places. BigInt rational arithmetic retains fractional cents until the reporting boundary; monetary rounding uses ties away from zero. Public exact values use numerator/denominator strings, so JSON serialization is safe. Percentages return integer basis points. Currency-specific decimal places are respected when parsing form amounts.

`FoodResult<T>` distinguishes available values from missing inputs, invalid denominators and inconsistent stock/waste evidence. Malformed quantities, negative costs, ambiguous units, duplicate rows, mixed currencies and overflow raise a typed validation error. Confirmed zero differs from blank. No exchange rate, density, cost coverage, customer identity or source connection is invented.

Primary exports are `convertFoodQuantity`, `costRecipe`, `theoreticalFoodCost`, `actualFoodCost`, `foodservicePeriodMetrics`, `foodWasteQuantityRate`, `tableTurns`, `validateFoodserviceContent` and `foodserviceRecordReport`. `FoodRecipe` carries currency, yielded portions and ingredient rows. `FoodPeriodEntry` carries explicitly reviewed inventory flows, sales, waste, labour, theoretical cost and closed checks.

The saved workspace uses one permitted location per record, source reference, source as-of date, recipe version or period dates, and optimistic record versions. The server derives tenant identity from authenticated membership, checks the selected industry capability and paid feature entitlement, checks location access on both old and new record scope, and enforces location currency. Recipe cost access requires inventory value permission. Period records additionally require revenue, profit and payroll-total permissions; writing periods requires payroll-edit permission. Records do not post journals or alter stock. Schema triggers independently reject cross-tenant location references and currency mismatches. Concurrent edits and duplicate recipe names or exact review periods return conflicts.

The first UI offers recipe ingredient entry, calculation preview, explicit review, persistent save/edit, source context and saved period summaries. The compact dashboard view reads saved periods only. It has no sample figures. It does not yet automatically map POS ingredients, expand nested recipes, allocate waste to causes, calculate table-turn time, or compare payroll policy definitions. These are explicit future data-model additions, not missing inputs silently replaced by zero.

## Verification evidence

The 23 focused domain and component tests passed on October 3, 2026. They cover exact mass/volume conversion, incompatible dimensions, malformed decimals, recipe yield, hydration, missing versus zero, fractional-cent aggregation, confirmed empty versus unknown production, duplicates, inventory credits/transfers, denominator scope, signed variance, unknown costs, currency mismatches, safe-integer overflow, waste consistency and table counts. Focused lint checks also passed.

The main implementation task reported both foodservice Worker/D1 flow tests passing after its fresh build. Those tests exercise authenticated save/reload, independently expected totals, duplicate and concurrent version conflicts, tenant/location isolation, currency enforcement, database triggers, restricted payroll visibility, revoked role/location access and cancelled subscription access. This is isolated integration evidence, not proof of production deployment. The loopback-only `scripts/industry-preview.mjs` additionally renders the real components with fictional café and dealership accounts against the built Worker and an isolated D1 database for browser form verification.
